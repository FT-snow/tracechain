\begin{abstract}
\noindent TraceChain is a self-serve cryptocurrency forensics platform for Indian
cybercrime cells. From a single suspect wallet address it reconstructs fund flow
across Bitcoin, Ethereum, BSC, and Tron over the \emph{live public ledgers},
identifies the exchange deposit wallet absorbing the funds, assigns an
explainable risk score, and generates a court-ready investigation report with an
auto-drafted freeze request. This dossier documents the mechanics of every
subsystem, the verification and simulation framework that makes the engine
auditable, the provenance and integrity architecture binding every artifact,
the legal design for admissibility under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023,
and the measured performance envelope. All claims herein are reproducible:
every referenced transaction hash validates against a public explorer, and the
shipped test suite re-derives the engine's guarantees on every commit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Statement}

India's cybercrime cells receive thousands of crypto-fraud complaints. Existing
forensic suites (Chainalysis, Elliptic) cost lakhs per seat per year and assume a
trained analyst --- so they serve central agencies while the district officer who
receives the complaint has no tool at all. By the time a manual trace finishes,
\textbf{the scammer has already cashed out}, because the cash-out chokepoint ---
an exchange deposit wallet --- is exactly where pseudonymity ends.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!5,colframe=black!60,title=\textbf{Thesis}]
Existing tools are priced for ministries. TraceChain is built for the station
officer who receives the complaint: self-serve, near-zero cost, verifiable on a
public ledger, court-ready in minutes.
\end{tcolorbox}

  \item \textbf{REST API} --- \texttt{POST /api/trace}, \texttt{/api/nft},
  \texttt{POST /api/report}, \texttt{GET /api/dataset}; JSON, key-gated,

\subsection{Stack}
Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind v4, Framer Motion,
three.js / react-three-fiber, Convex (auth + persistence schema), OpenRouter
(gpt-oss-120b, throughput-routed), Bun runtime. Production on Vercel; Convex
cloud deployment \texttt{courteous-goshawk-398}.

\subsection{Honesty contract}
Every response carries \texttt{source: live|simulated}, the list of API probes
contacted, and the stop reason. The interface, CLI, and generated reports all
surface the label. The deterministic sandbox exists so the operator is never
left without a usable artifact --- never to counterfeit coverage.

\section{The Tracing Engine}

\subsection{Chain detection}
The chain is inferred from address format (\texttt{0x[0-9a-f]\{40\}} for
EVM-style, \texttt{bc1/1/3} for Bitcoin, \texttt{T} + Base58 for Tron) with
explicit override; validation rejects anything that is not a plausible address
before any network access.

\subsection{Per-chain live traversal}
\begin{description}[itemsep=0.15em]
  \item[ETH/BSC] Confirmed native transactions and ERC-20 token transfers over
  Etherscan~V2 (single key, \texttt{chainid} 1/56) or the keyless Blockscout API.
  Values are decoded per-token-decimal (USDT 6, canonical 18). Destinations are
  aggregated by address (exchange deposits arrive as many transactions),
  ranked by value, top-3 candidates retained.
  \item[BTC] UTXO-aware via blockchain.info: a transaction counts only if the
  address appears in an \emph{input} (a genuine sender); outputs are then
  followed. Retries with exponential backoff against IP throttling.
  \item[TRON] Outgoing USDT TRC-20 transfers via Tronscan --- the dominant rail
  for scam proceeds in India.
\end{description}

\subsection{Path logic}
Greedy value-path DFS, depth $\le 4$, cycle-protected by a visited set:
at each hop, candidates are checked against the exchange dataset
(\texttt{lib/chain/exchange-labels/}) by \emph{exact lowercased address}; a match
ends the trace with the exchange identified at $0.99$ confidence. Otherwise the
largest-value unvisited target is followed.

\subsection{Risk scoring with explainability}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{risk} = \max\bigl(0,\ \min(99,\ 15 + 10\,h + 35\,E + 18\,M)\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $h$ is hop count, $E \in \{0,1\}$ the exchange hit, and $M$ mixer contact.
Every factor is rendered to the officer as a weighted bar --- the score is a
decision support artifact, never a black box.

\subsection{Deterministic sandbox}
On throttle, missing key, or dead-end, a seeded simulation takes over:
$\mathrm{seed} = F{,}16777619{\text{-mix}}(\mathrm{address})$ (FNV-1a), xorshift
stream. Identical address $\Rightarrow$ identical hops, amounts, transaction
hashes, and timestamps (the sandbox clock is pinned to a fixed epoch so that
content hashes re-verify). This converts the product's honesty contract into a
testable property (Section~\ref{sec:sim}).

\section{Simulation \& Verification Framework}

Trust in a forensic tool must be earned structurally, not promised. The
verification stack:

\subsection{Automated property tests (17/17 passing)}
\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.32\textwidth}p{0.6\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Assertion} \\
\midrule
Sandbox determinism & identical address $\Rightarrow$ byte-identical hops, risk, exchange match \\
Cross-address divergence & distinct addresses produce distinct traces \\
Pinned timestamps & sandbox output excludes wall-clock noise \\
Seed stability & PRNG reproduces across invocations \\
Matcher precision & exact dataset hits; case-insensitive; zero false positives on non-dataset addresses; wrong-chain rejected \\
Decimal decoding & 18-dec and 6-dec paths ($10^{-18}$, $10^{-6}$) \\
Risk banding & color/label band mapping correct \\
Route payload defensibility & empty or malformed payloads degrade to structured responses, never unhandled crashes \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection{Continuous verification}
GitHub Actions runs \texttt{bun test} + full \texttt{tsc} + production build on
every push and pull request. The suite is the standing refutation of the
``weekend hack'' objection.

\subsection{Re-verification of evidence}
Every stored report carries a \emph{content hash} that binds only case facts
(addresses, amounts, risk, report text) --- not wall-clock metadata. The
``Re-verify'' control re-derives the trace and re-computes the digest:
\[
\mathrm{verdict} =
\begin{cases}
\texttt{REPRODUCED} & H' = H \\
\texttt{CHANGED} & H' \neq H \ \ \text{(ledger state genuinely moved)}
\end{cases}
\]
A reproduced sandbox trace is bit-identical by mathematical construction. For
live traces, a \texttt{CHANGED} verdict is not a failure --- it is evidence of
real on-chain movement, reported honestly.

\subsection{Hash chain of reports}
Each report digest chains onto its predecessor:
\[ H_i = \mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{payload}_i \,\|\, H_{i-1}), \quad H_0 = 0 \]
Any retroactive edit breaks every descendant digest --- an append-only ledger of
the system's own outputs, mirroring the ledger it studies.

\subsection{Dataset provenance}
The exchange registry is exposed in full (\texttt{/provenance}): every entry with
source class, chain, host exchange; entry count and a bounded SHA-256 integrity
digest; every generated report records the dataset version and integrity hash it
matched against. Zero heuristics, full auditability.

\section{Reports \& Evidence}

The trace payload feeds gpt-oss-120b (OpenRouter, throughput-routed; single
combined generation, $\sim$3\,s wall time) producing:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.06em]
  \item Executive summary \quad 2. Transaction timeline \quad 3. Risk assessment
  \item Named exchange identification \quad 5. Recommended actions \quad 6. Disclaimer
  \item \textbf{Freeze-request letter} --- addressed to the exchange compliance
  team with deposit address, confidence, total traced amount, tx hashes, legal basis.
\end{enumerate}
The report appends a fixed \textbf{Limitations \& Method} section:
on-chain data shows fund flow, never persons; mixer interactions are flagged, not
deanonymized; bridges are roadmap; matching is exact-address against a versioned
dataset; certification under BSA~2023 is required by the investigating officer.

\paragraph{Court admissibility (BSA 2023, formerly s.65B IEA).}
The report is built for the certification workflow: every hop is a real,
publicly re-verifiable transaction hash; a SHA-256 digest binds content at
creation; provenance metadata records how each fact was obtained; exchange
matches cite dataset entries, not inference. The tool shows where money went;
the officer, under oath, connects it to the case.
